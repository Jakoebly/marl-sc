\chapter{Problem Formulation}
\label{chap:problem-formulation}


Customer demand is the only exogenous stochastic driver of the system and is
generated independently for each region in every period. The model departs from
the aggregate per-region demand rate used in most prior multi-agent inventory
studies [\textbf{CITE: MARL inventory work that models demand as an aggregate per-period quantity per region or SKU}] and instead represents demand at the level of individual customer orders. An order is a single request that may span several \glspl{acr:sku} at once, and it is the unit at which the network allocatesfulfilment and incurs outbound shipment cost. Representing demand at the order
level rather than as an aggregate quantity per \gls{acr:sku} is therefore not a
cosmetic choice. It is what allows the fulfilment logic of
Section~\ref{sec:fulfillment} to charge shipment cost per dispatched order and to
split a single order across warehouses, and it is one of the features that
distinguishes the present environment from the symmetric single-quantity
formulations reviewed in Chapter~\ref{chap:background}.
