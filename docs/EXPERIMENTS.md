# Experimental Program and Statistical Protocol

This document describes the thesis's experimental program, the statistical protocol used to analyse its results, and the supplementary appendix experiments. Every experiment specifies a procedure, a deliverable, an expected outcome (pre-registered hypothesis), and a falsifier (the empirical pattern that would force a different conclusion), so that both confirmatory and negative-result outcomes are committed in advance.

For thesis scope, research questions, contribution claims, environment design, algorithms, baselines, and interventions, see [SCOPE.md](SCOPE.md). For what the system models and how its components behave, see [SYSTEM_MODEL.md](SYSTEM_MODEL.md). For runtime wiring, configuration, and implementation details, see [IMPLEMENTATION.md](IMPLEMENTATION.md).

## 1. Experimental Program

The program is organized into five blocks:

- **E0** establishes the baseline
- **E1** is the diagnostic study
- **E2** tests the interventions
- **E3** ablates and explores secondary axes
- **E4** covers scalability and transfer

Every experiment group specifies a purpose and the contribution it supports. Every experiment within a group specifies a procedure, a deliverable, an expected outcome (pre-registered hypothesis), and a falsifier (the empirical pattern that would force a different conclusion). Deliverables are not the final plots that go into the thesis but just the outcome of the single isolated experiment. Falsifiers exist so a negative result is still a publishable result.

**Three-alternative framing for the heterogeneity-induced gap.** E0 establishes the symmetric reference and E1 quantifies the heterogeneity-induced gap that motivates the thesis. After E1 the gap is empirically established but unexplained. Three alternative explanations for the gap are considered in the remainder of the thesis: 
1. **Insufficient hyperparameter tuning:** the gap is an artefact of HPs tuned on the symmetric env that fail to transfer. Tested by per-env re-tuning in E1.3 and E1.4. 
2. **Architectural misalignment:** the gap reflects a structural limitation of the shared-policy architecture under heterogeneous local contexts. Tested by interventions in E2 and decomposed in E3.1.
3. **Optimisation-trajectory difficulty:** the gap reflects an exploration or curvature problem that any sufficient training-procedure tweak would close. Tested by warm-start and curriculum in E3.5. 

The thesis pre-registers 2 as the dominant explanation. The experiment plan tests all three so the architectural claim of C3 rests on three legs and not only on the success of 2: the failure of 1 to close the gap in E1.4, the success of 2 in E2 and E3.1, and the demonstration in E3.5 that 3 is at most complementary to 2 rather than a substitute for it. A clean falsification of 1 and 3 is one possible outcome; the more likely outcome is that 1 contributes nothing measurable and 3 contributes a secondary effect that does not by itself recover the gap.

### E0 — Symmetric Baseline

**Purpose.** Establish that all five RL algorithms (IPPO-PS, IPPO-noPS, MAPPO-PS, MAPPO-noPS, CPPO) converge to comparable performance on the symmetric environment. The symmetric env is a sanity check and not a result. Under weak coupling the per-warehouse problem is near-decomposable, so centralization and parameter sharing should both provide no measurable benefit. The noPS rows (E0.2, E0.4) empirically anchor the symmetric reference for the 2×2 PS/noPS grid that becomes diagnostically informative only under heterogeneity in E1.4.

### E2 — Targeted Interventions

**Purpose.** Test each intervention individually and as the best per-env combination on the four heterogeneous envs against the locked E1.4 MAPPO-PS baseline. All experiments use MAPPO-PS unless noted.

**Uniform re-tune protocol:** Every E2 cell receives a 100-trial Optuna re-tune on seed 42 over the same three HPs: `lr`, `entropy_coeff`, and `vf_loss_coeff`. Each HP is sampled log-uniformly in `[E1.4-MAPPO-PS-best / 3, E1.4-MAPPO-PS-best × 3]`. All other HPs are inherited from the env-specific E1.4-best MAPPO-PS configuration. The top-5 trials are seed-evaluated across the 10 default root seeds from §2 and the per-seed-mean best is locked as the env-specific config. If Optuna's chosen optimum for any HP falls in the outer 10% of its log-uniform range, that side is expanded by 3× and the re-tune is re-run. This guards against the assumption that the E1.4 optimum is a good anchor for the intervention's optimum. Due to the reduced number of samples, the TPE sampler reduces `n_startup_trials` from the default 50 to 25. 

**Why only these three HPs for the re-tune:** The re-tune is a local correction for HPs that the intervention plausibly perturbs, not a full re-tune. `lr` is the universal first-order knob whose optimum can shift whenever the gradient landscape changes (input scale shifts for A1, new features for A2, reward scale for A3, new architecture for A4). `entropy_coeff` controls exploration and is sensitive to changes in action-gradient magnitude. `vf_loss_coeff` controls the actor/critic gradient balance and is particularly relevant for A3 (reward rescaling shifts value-target magnitudes) and A4 (new critic representation). Re-tuning more HPs would require a much larger trial budget per cell, which compute does currently not allow and is also not warrented given the extensive 1000-sample tunes in E1.

**Why not tune architecture HPs for A4:** The attention architecture is fixed at `d_model = 64` (a standard small-attention embedding dimension that gives meaningful per-SKU representational capacity at five tokens without inflating the parameter count, which grows with `d_model²` and stays independent of the SKU count), `n_heads = 2` (the lowest head count that still enables multi-head behaviour, because more heads would split the five-token attention into per-head subspaces too small to be useful), `ff_hidden = 64` (a minimal feed-forward width at one times `d_model`, kept small because five tokens do not warrant the conventional fourfold expansion), and `head_hidden_sizes = [128]` for the actor and `[64]` for the critic (a single shallow read-out layer that keeps the representational work in the attention stage rather than in the head). The actor head is the wider of the two to preserve resolution across the five per-SKU action means, whereas the critic head can be narrower because it maps an already larger global-observation input to a single scalar value. Fixing the architecture rather than tuning it preserves a symmetric HP-tuning budget across all interventions, allows the matched-capacity control in E2.5 to target a single architecture per env, and reflects the thesis's claim that the value of A4 derives from the per-SKU tokenisation mechanism rather than from architectural search. Sensitivity to the architectural choice itself is reported in appendix §3.1.

**A5 composition rule (per env).** A5 is composed independently for each heterogeneous env. The default composition on env `e` is the union `{A1, A2, A3, A4}`. Intervention `A_i` is dropped from A5 on env `e` iff, on env `e` itself, its paired difference vs. the E1.4 MAPPO-PS baseline has both (a) mean < −2% normalized score and (b) 95% paired CI strictly below zero. The rule is conservative by design: failing only one condition keeps the intervention in. E2.6 reports up to four (possibly different) A5 compositions, one per hetero env, and the per-env composition table is itself an input to the C4 decision matrix.

**Statistical analysis:** All E2 comparisons use the paired-difference protocol in §2. The five pre-registered primary tests are corrected via Holm-Bonferroni at family-wise α = 0.05:

|---|---|---|

SCOPE.md §7 already states a directional hypothesis for every cell, but only the five primary cells are held to the corrected significance threshold; readers wishing to apply a 16-test correction can compute it from the reported values.

## 2. Statistical Protocol

The statistical protocol below is committed in advance.

- **Seeds.** Default `[100, 200, ..., 1000]` (n = 10) for E0, E1, E2, E3, E4 and the §3 appendix experiments. If compute is too tight, change to n = 3 for the secondary and exploratory experiments in E3, E4, and the §3 appendix experiments. Same seeds across conditions within an experiment so that comparisons are paired.
- **Primary comparison (paired differences via across-seed statistics).** Comparisons between condition `A` and condition `B` use the same seeds across both, so per-seed scores `A_s` and `B_s` are paired. Across the `n` seeds compute `mean(A)`, `mean(B)`, `σ²(A)`, `σ²(B)`, and `Cov(A, B)`. Report:
  - point estimate: `Δ = mean(A) − mean(B)`
  - paired variance: `σ²_paired = σ²(A) + σ²(B) − 2 · Cov(A, B)`
  - paired SE: `SE_paired = √(σ²_paired) / √n`
  - paired t-statistic: `t = Δ / SE_paired` with `n − 1` degrees of freedom
  - effect size: `Cohen's d_z = Δ / √(σ²_paired)`

  The covariance term absorbs the seed-induced noise shared between `A` and `B`, so `σ_paired` is materially smaller than the unpaired `√(σ²(A) + σ²(B))` whenever the conditions are run on the same seeds. Failing to use paired statistics inflates the standard error and weakens detected effects.
- **Significance threshold and multiple-comparison correction.** Family-wise α = 0.05. Each experiment block pre-declares a small set of primary tests, corrected via Holm-Bonferroni; all other cells are reported as secondary with uncorrected p-values and 95% paired CIs but are not held to the corrected threshold.
  - **E2 primary tests (k = 5):** each intervention vs. E1.4 MAPPO-PS on its hypothesized primary-target env(s) — A1 → `env_demand_hetero`, A1 → `env_cost_hetero`, A2 → `env_sku_hetero`, A3 → `env_sku_hetero`, A4 → `env_combined_hetero` (see §1 E2 for the rationale; A1 has two primary tests because SCOPE.md §7 lists both demand heterogeneity and cost asymmetry as A1's primary targets).
  - **E1 primary tests (k = 4 per algorithm):** each algorithm's re-tuned vs. transferred result on the four hetero envs.
  - The remaining cells in SCOPE.md §7 and §1 have directional hypotheses but are not in the primary set; readers who prefer a fully corrected analysis can compute it from the reported uncorrected p-values.
- **Additivity test for A5 (E2.6).** For each hetero env, define `γ = Δ(A5) − Σ_{A_i ∈ A5_e} Δ(A_i)`, computed from the per-condition across-seed means (point estimate) and the paired covariance matrix of the five conditions' per-seed scores (CI). Report `γ ± SE(γ)` with a 95% paired CI. CI ⊃ 0 ⇒ additive composition; CI strictly below 0 ⇒ substitutive; CI strictly above 0 ⇒ synergistic. The C3 additivity claim is pre-registered to this test on `env_combined_hetero`.
- **Headline metric.** `eval/episode_return_mean` averaged across 100 evaluation episodes per seed, evaluated with a deterministic policy (mean action). Per-SKU inventory levels, stockout rates, and cost-component breakdowns are reported alongside but are not the primary statistic.
- **Normalized score.** For cross-env comparisons, report `(metric(condition) − metric(Random)) / (metric(BS-Optimized) − metric(Random))` so degradation is comparable across envs with different absolute cost scales.
- **Pre-registered vs. exploratory.** The expected outcomes and falsifiers in §1 are pre-registered hypotheses; per-SKU behaviour analysis (E3.2) and attention-weight visualisation (E3.2) are exploratory and labelled as such in the thesis.
- **Reproducibility.** All runs use the central `SeedManager` (see [IMPLEMENTATION.md §7](IMPLEMENTATION.md)). PyTorch deterministic mode is enabled. Per-(worker, env) seeds are derived from the root seed.

### 3.3 Factored Per-(Warehouse, SKU) Policy

This subsection is run only if time allows. It addresses the reviewer-anticipated question "why not just factor the policy across SKUs?" by comparing A4's per-SKU self-attention against a strictly factored alternative that processes each (warehouse, SKU) pair independently with shared parameters and so has no architectural channel for inter-SKU information.

**Procedure.** Replace A4's attention backbone with a factored architecture in which the shared policy is invoked once per (warehouse, SKU) pair. Each call sees only that SKU's inventory, pipeline, demand-forecast, and rolling-mean features plus the warehouse-level aggregate features and the warehouse one-hot. No cross-SKU information is available to the policy by construction. Train on all four hetero envs using the locked E2.4 per-env config; no re-tune. n = 3 seeds (`[100, 200, 300]`).

**Three layers of evidence for the loss-of-inter-SKU-information claim.**

1. *Performance gap.* Factored vs A4 paired-difference normalized score per env.
2. *Cross-SKU action dependence (partial-dependence analysis).* On 200 held-out evaluation rollout states per env, perturb the inventory of SKU_j and measure the change in the action mean for SKU_i (i ≠ j) under A4 and under the factored policy. A4 should produce non-zero dependence; the factored policy is structurally zero.
3. *Attention-weight off-diagonal mass.* Reuse the A4 attention heatmap from E3.2 and report the mean off-diagonal-to-diagonal attention-weight ratio per env. A non-trivial ratio on `env_sku_hetero` and `env_combined_hetero` independently corroborates the cross-SKU-coordination interpretation; a near-zero ratio would falsify that interpretation independently of the factored result.

**Reporting.** Per-env factored-vs-A4 paired-difference table; per-env partial-dependence plot for A4 vs factored; per-env attention off-diagonal-to-diagonal ratio.

**Expected outcome.** The factored policy underperforms A4 on `env_sku_hetero` and `env_combined_hetero` by ≥ 5% normalized score with paired CI excluding zero, and is approximately neutral on `env_demand_hetero` and `env_cost_hetero` where the A4 gain in E2.4 is itself small. The partial-dependence analysis shows non-zero cross-SKU dependence in A4 and zero in the factored policy.

**Falsifier.** Factored ≈ A4 on both `env_sku_hetero` and `env_combined_hetero` → A4's value comes from per-SKU symmetry alone rather than inter-SKU coordination, and the mechanism story for A4 in C3 weakens from "cross-SKU attention" to "explicit per-SKU structure suffices."

### 3.5 Temporal Architecture Check

This subsection is run only if time allows.
